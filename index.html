🏔️ Altitude

Steamboat sits at 6,732 ft, and several hikes on this board climb well past that — Devil's Causeway and the Zirkel hikes top out near 10,500–12,000 ft, Chasm Lake and Isabelle Glacier go even higher. That's a real jump if you're coming from closer to sea level, and it hits kids differently than adults — often as crankiness, poor sleep, headache, or loss of appetite rather than the "can't breathe" feeling adults notice first.

  • Ease in: keep Sept 25–26 light — the Core Trail, Botanic Park, or a short Fish Creek Falls walk, not Devil's Causeway or Mount Zirkel. Altitude symptoms usually show up in the first 24–48 hours.
  • Hydrate before you climb: more than feels necessary, starting the day before a big hike. Alcohol and caffeine make it worse.
  • Watch the kiddo, not just yourselves: a 4-year-old can't always describe "I feel off" — watch for unusual fussiness, refusing food, or bad sleep that first night or two.
  • When to actually worry: persistent vomiting, confusion, trouble breathing at rest, or symptoms that get worse instead of better — that's a "descend and get checked" situation, not a "push through it" one.

🐻 Wildlife Safety

Several picks on this board are genuine bear and moose country — Buffalo Pass, the Zirkel Wilderness trailheads, and the Grand Lake area especially. I've mentioned "good moose odds" a couple times without ever addressing what that means practically — worth fixing.

  • Bears: make noise on trail (talking is enough, bear bells are optional), never leave food or trash unattended at a trailhead or campsite, and store snacks in a sealed bag inside a pack, not a stroller pocket.
  • Moose — genuinely more dangerous at close range than bears: they don't fear humans and will charge if they feel crowded, especially a cow with a calf. Give any moose at least 50 yards, more if a calf is present. If one is on the trail ahead, turn around rather than trying to pass.
  • If you encounter either: stay calm, don't run, back away slowly while keeping the animal in view. For a bear, make yourself look bigger and back away; running can trigger a chase response.
  • With a 4-year-old specifically: keep them within arm's reach on any trail in bear/moose country rather than letting them range ahead, and do a quick "if we see a big animal, we stay quiet and walk backward with Mom/Dad" talk before you set out.

📵 Cell & GPS Dead Zones

The Directions buttons throughout this board are only useful while you have signal — and several of the drives and trailheads genuinely don't.

  • Known weak/no-signal stretches: Seedhouse Road out to the Slavonia trailhead (Gilpin Lake, Gold Creek Lake), Buffalo Pass Road, most of County Road 129 north of Clark, and long stretches of CO-131 between Steamboat and Yampa.
  • Before you leave town each day: download that day's route as an offline Google Maps area (Google Maps app → Offline Maps), and download the specific AllTrails map for any hike linked on this board — both work with zero signal once saved.
  • Basic backcountry habit: tell someone (or just each other, out loud) which trailhead you're headed to and roughly when you expect to be back, especially for the longer hikes like Devil's Causeway or Mount Zirkel-area trails.

🎒 Packing List

The real math: 3 free bags per person × 3 of you = 9 total with United, minus 2 bike cases = 7 free bags to split across the three of you for hiking gear, hot springs stuff, wedding attire, and layers for a 40°F+ swing. That's genuinely comfortable, not a tight squeeze — worth organizing by category rather than worrying about volume.

Suggested split: with 7 bags for 3 people, a natural approach is one bag per person for everyday/hiking clothes, plus 1–2 shared bags for the categories below — one for hot springs gear (swimsuits, water shoes, towels), one for wedding attire (or just a garment bag, which typically doesn't count against your allowance anyway). No need to cram everything into one bag or wear your boots through security unless you'd rather travel light regardless.

RMNP NOTE — Grand Lake/Coyote Valley/Adams Falls are on RMNP's west side, ~2 hrs (94 mi) from Steamboat — comfortably within a 3-hour radius, and a genuinely easy day trip.
